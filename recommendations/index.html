Recommendations

games, music, films, books …

Games, music, films, books and other things I find great, with a few words on why.

film · 2022

Aftersun

by Charlotte Wells

A film about memory that feels like an old, worn-down home video… and the gaps are where it hurts. The strobe-lit dance near the end, I was NOT ready for it. Still not ready.

music

Nick Drake

Strange tunings, very few words, a lot of space. Pink Moon is barely half an hour long and still feels endless. Music for the hour after everyone has gone home.

music

Robbie Basho

The steel-string guitar as a sacred instrument. Open tunings, ragas, a voice that goes for everything at once… Proof that you can take the acoustic guitar completely, almost embarrassingly seriously. I love that.

music

Adrianne Lenker

Her fingerpicking bends time too, in a gentler way. Songs that feel found rather than written. On songs and instrumentals you can hear the cabin, the rain, the wood… the room is part of the music, and you know I care about rooms.

music

Godspeed You! Black Emperor

Pieces that rise and fall like weather. Field recordings, tremolo guitars, strings, long long crescendos… music as protest and as ritual at the same time. They showed me how long you can make people wait, and how good the waiting can feel.

music

Black Country, New Road

Songs that build up, collapse and build up again… time is malleable here too, but with a whole band piling saxophone, violin and piano on top of each other. Ants From Up There is one of the most beautiful albums of the last years. Basta.

music

Elliott Smith

Whispered songs that sound like they were recorded at three in the morning in an empty flat. The double-tracked voice, chords that never land where you expect them to… Either/Or still gets me every single time.

music

Matti Kutzner

A drummer and a friend. Watch his hands if you ever get the chance… it's worth it.

music

Panto Parzo

The band of a friend of mine. Go listen, and go see them live when you can!

film · 1977

Eraserhead

by David Lynch

The SOUND of it. The industrial hum that never stops, rooms that seem to breathe… you could watch this film with your eyes closed and still have nightmares. One of the reasons I think of sound as a place and not only as something you hear.

music

Raphael Rogiński

Among many art forms, I love how time can be manipulated and bent. I believe this happens in Rogiński's brilliant guitar playing and arranging too. Seeing him play live twice changed the way I use the guitar myself, and now I incorporate “prepared” guitar into my playing a lot as well.

music

Kali Malone

Again, time is malleable here. She also introduced me to the pipe organ properly and made me want to use it. Ohhh, and this is where I found my fascination with temperaments as well.

music

Still House Plants

Time is malleable AGAIN… but this time in a groovy sense, with things twisting and turning. The way Jess, Finlay and David interlock is magical. The grooviest music, I always want to dance to it.

game · 2019

Disco Elysium

by ZA/UM

Maybe one of THE greatest artworks created in any medium, it's that good. Harry Du Bois and his plethora of voices now live inside my head too.

I NEED YOU. YOU CAN KEEP ME ON THIS EARTH. BE VIGILANT. I LOVE YOU.

performance inspiration

Lingua Ignota

She introduced me to the Sacred Harp tradition and its amazing songs. Maybe nothing on this list is as harrowing as her musical work. Unfortunately I missed her live show in Berlin because of y’know who.

performance inspiration

Otay:onii

Seeing her roll and move gracefully around me at MS Stubnitz, with at most ten other people in the audience, opened my eyes to how I NEED to perform as well. No barrier between artist and audience, the abolition of the stage… I will always cherish this experience as a formative one.

film · 1966

Andrei Rublev

by Andrei Tarkovsky

The film behind Mariia III. Faith, suffering and making art treated as one and the same struggle… and then the bell. A boy betting his life on a sound nobody has heard yet. I think about that chapter whenever I sit down at an organ I don't know.

book · 2009

My Struggle

by Karl Ove Knausgård

Prose and poetry woven together in astonishing ways. I don't remember if there is a plot to this book, but it doesn't need one.

film · 1997

Neon Genesis Evangelion: The End of Evangelion

by Hideaki Anno

Grief, death and religion, all of it at once and at full volume. Bach and the end of the world in the same breath. Beautiful and almost unbearable, I can't think of another film that hurts in quite this way.

film · 1969

The Color of Pomegranates

by Sergei Parajanov

Hypnotic… the sounds… the shots… I have watched it about ten times already, and countless more times just to fall asleep to it and have vivid dreams.

book · 1973

Gravity's Rainbow

by Thomas Pynchon

What a monolith. The most well-written prose, washing over me in streams of varying degrees of disgusting and pleasurable elements.

music

Shearling

The previous release of Sprains The Lamb as Effigy was the biggest inspiration for me when I made my first album. Shearling's new music is just as intense as the work back then, and seeing them live at Hinterraum (and being able to take pictures for them) was one of the first things I did in Berlin after I moved there. Back then it felt like this city could promise me great things.

I also found out after the fact that a person I now consider a good friend (who also has a band called Husbandry and played in the same venue on a different date) was at this exact concert. We found out we had been at the concert together when we met in a seminar at university.

book · 1931

The Waves

by Virginia Woolf

Prose and poetry woven together in astonishing ways. I don't remember if there is a plot to this book, but it doesn't need one.

music

Jon Collin

A somewhat similar but also dissimilar approach to guitar than Rogiński's. Burnt Monday is a great album for a moody, foggy day. Or for taking the train from Berlin to Warsaw.
